Personal study guide | 4 November 2026

Six papers to study first

You do not need to work through all 19 papers before forming a direction. The first three address the immediate function-discovery problem. The next three support alternative representations and eventual learned decomposition.

If you only read three: sinusoidal optimization, exhaustive SR/MDL, and RAG-SR. The first pass below is about 35 PDF pages; detailed derivations and reference sections can wait. Section references are PDF page numbers, not journal pagination.

1 | Read now

Sinusoidal Frequency Estimation by Gradient Descent

ICASSP | 2023

Start with: PDF p.2, Fig.1 and Section 2.1; pp.3-4 experiments

Read to answer: Why can an exactly suitable oscillator still fail to fit? What does the surrogate improve, and what failures remain?

Connection to our work: Use damping as a temporary optimization aid, then restore/refit the transmitted real expression. Test random phases, chirps, weak components and 640-sample signals at practical budgets before adopting it.

Keep in mind: No formal general convergence result; not yet a general speech/chirped-AM-FM solution. Final error must be measured on the intended real expression, not the damped surrogate.

2 | Read now

(Exhaustive) symbolic regression and model selection by minimum description length

Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A | 2026

Start with: PDF pp.3-7; skim pp.12-13 afterwards

Read to answer: What must we count to call one mathematical description smaller, and what does exhaustive structure search actually guarantee?

Connection to our work: Add safe canonical simplification, remove redundant scale/constant parameters, and enumerate very small programs as a coverage reference. Keep parameter-count frontiers now and report actual representation cost separately; precision-aware coding can follow later without RVQ.

Keep in mind: Enumeration coverage is bounded by grammar and maximum complexity; local constant fitting does not become globally solved through enumeration. MDL depends on coding/model assumptions and operator vocabulary. Its statistical precision formula should not be transplanted blindly to noiseless speech coding.

3 | Read now

RAG-SR: Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Neural Symbolic Regression

ICLR | 2025

Start with: PDF pp.4-9; Appendix C/D on pp.17-19 as needed

Read to answer: How can the model repair a partially correct formula from the residual, rather than generating everything again?

Connection to our work: Condition expression edits on the current curve and its residual, not only raw IMF samples. Reuse good subexpressions, replace rather than blindly append, and retrain on actual successful speech-derived fits. Keep test speakers out of the training library.

Keep in mind: The model is multi-feature symbolic regression, not short speech IMF coding. Edit distance to a generating tree is not our primary objective. Residual addition without replacement can bloat descriptions.

4 | Read next

Coupled Splines for Sparse Curve Fitting

IEEE Transactions on Image Processing | 2022

Start with: PDF pp.2-5 and experimental comparisons on pp.7-8

Read to answer: Can adaptive shared knots describe two related curves cheaply? Which assumptions belong specifically to periodic 2D contours?

Connection to our work: Fit the real and imaginary parts of a demodulated analytic IMF as coupled envelopes with shared adaptive knots. Phase rotation rotates these coordinates, suggesting a natural use of rotation-invariant coupling. Count knot locations, both coefficient sets and carrier parameters.

Keep in mind: Theorems concern the declared 2D periodic convex objective, not arbitrary AM-FM factorization or shortest speech programs. Nonperiodic speech boundaries and sparse knot payloads need separate treatment.

5 | Before learned decomposition

Variational generalized nonlinear mode decomposition: Algorithm and applications

Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing | 2024

Start with: PDF pp.3-8; conclusion on p.19

Read to answer: Can demodulation make a mode easier to describe, and what happens when ridge-separation assumptions fail?

Connection to our work: Generate programs for demodulated quadrature envelopes or phase innovations; test a frequency-domain representation for transient modes. Count carrier, support boundaries, coordinate choice, and envelope coefficients. Select by achieved description/error, not ridge-type rules alone.

Keep in mind: No compact parameter transmission or speech coding experiment. Demodulated amplitude/phase trajectories remain functions, not automatically short vectors. The ridge classification and thresholds are algorithmic choices.

6 | Before learned decomposition

TFD-Net: Towards Intelligent Time-Frequency Mode Decomposition with Practical Applications

AAAI | 2026

Start with: PDF pp.2-4, Fig.1; results and conclusion on pp.5-7

Read to answer: Which pieces are learned, what is supervised, and which reconstruction steps remain iterative?

Connection to our work: Borrow permutation-invariant query slots and frequency-track proposals to initialize VMD-like optimization. Add actual program reconstruction and complexity objectives; validate on speech and variable mode counts.

Keep in mind: The existing training does not optimize the size of a transmitted expression. Its conclusion identifies unfolded reconstruction as future work; it should not be described as already a fully neural end-to-end waveform decomposition.

Read selectively afterwards

Symbolic Regression via MDLformer-Guided Search: From Minimizing Prediction Error to Minimizing Description Length - After RAG-SR. PDF pp.3-7 and Appendix C on p.16. A learned guide for remaining formula complexity; inspect training scale and distinguish predicted token length from real coding cost.

WIRE: Wavelet Implicit Neural Representations - For localized oscillatory functions. PDF pp.3-5. How localization changes representation and fitting bias; visual results are not direct speech evidence.

FINER++: Building a Family of Variable-periodic Functions for Activating Implicit Neural Representation - For activation-language extensions. PDF pp.5-8. Variable-periodic functions and initialization. Prefer this over reading both FINER versions initially.

KAN: Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks - Optional spline-to-symbolic route. PDF pp.3-5 and Appendix M, p.32. Learned edge functions and pruning; symbolification still uses a candidate library.

Keep the broad symbolic-regression survey as a reference. Defer MAPSS until we revisit perceptual evaluation on reconstructed speech in context. The two learnable-EMD papers and DAMD/STVMD are useful comparisons after the function-fitting objective is clearer.

Questions worth writing down while reading

  1. What exactly is stored and evaluated: a complete formula, a spline, or shared neural weights?
  2. Does the method improve the function family, the search, or only numerical optimization?
  3. What experimental result supports its claim, and on which signals?
  4. Which assumptions could fail for a 640-sample speech IMF?
  5. What single controlled ablation would let us accept or reject the transfer?

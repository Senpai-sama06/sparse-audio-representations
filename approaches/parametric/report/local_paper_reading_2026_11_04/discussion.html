Research discussion record | 4 November 2026

Compact IMF function discovery

Recommendation: improve function discovery on frozen VMD modes first. Then train decomposition to produce modes that the real mathematical representation can describe cheaply.

Review of all 19 supplied PDFs, focused on methods, objectives, empirical evidence and relevant appendices. This is not a line-by-line verification of every appendix proof. Original PDFs remain unchanged. Proposed transfers and algebraic audits below are our analysis, not reported speech results.

Connection to our completed pilot

The held-out pilot covers 64 IMFs. Mean best-found NMSE is 0.8264 for random grammar, 0.6773 after synthetic pretraining and 0.6486 after adaptation. Only 1/64 adapted results reaches NMSE 0.01. These are finite-search results, not a limit on representability. Equal proposal counts also do not imply equal wall time.

Code inspection confirms: whole-expression MLP/GRU proposals, no residual-conditioned structural edits, a finite elementary grammar, bounded float64 local constant fitting, a separately counted output gain, and no reusable expression graph. The current study excludes the VMD residual and does not establish full-block or perceptual reconstruction. No training or implementation changes were made for this review.

Four proposed directions

1. Discover functions in coordinates that expose simple structure

For a mode u(t), compare a direct expression with A(t) cos(phi(t)) and with p(t) cos(psi(t)) + q(t) sin(psi(t)). VGNMD motivates demodulation; coupled splines motivate joint sparse fitting of p and q. A carrier psi removes rapid rotation while p and q describe the remaining slow changes. Unlike amplitude/unwrapped phase, quadrature coordinates do not require dividing by amplitude at zeros. Finite-block analytic-signal estimation and endpoint extensions can still introduce artifacts.

Our proposed extension. Learn compositions for the envelopes and carrier, rather than choosing a complete function from a fixed family menu. The carrier can itself be a generated expression. Count all carrier constants, envelope constants, knots, boundaries, operators and any stored transform choice. A coordinate change does not guarantee compression: only a lower fully counted description at the same original-waveform error establishes a benefit.

Falsifiable experiment. First use the same frozen 40-ms VMD modes. Compare direct, amplitude-phase and quadrature descriptions, with direct fitting retained as an escape route. Test random-phase chirps, amplitude zeros, beating and localized bursts before speech. Measure original mode NMSE, boundary/interior error, size and fit success versus compute. Test shared versus independent envelope knots without transferring the periodic-contour theorem to nonperiodic speech.

2. Learn to repair and compose programs, with a real search teacher

RAG-SR supplies residual-conditioned replacement and an archive of evaluated subexpressions. MDLformer suggests learning which partial expression leaves an easier remaining problem. The current pilot samples complete expressions and only refits their constants; it cannot repair a promising partial result during that search.

Our proposed extension. Condition an editing model on the IMF, current expression, current reconstruction and residual. Retrieve a useful partial program, edit or replace a subtree, refit constants and retain the measured Pareto front. Train on successful teacher-search trajectories, including failures and near misses, rather than only random synthetic trees. Keep an exploration arm so a learned cost estimate cannot veto unfamiliar structures.

Falsifiable experiment. Compare complete-program generation, residual edits, retrieval-only and retrieval-plus-edits under both equal proposals and equal wall time. Freeze the training archive before held-out speakers. Inspect actual error versus cost; do not treat token edit distance, a neural cost estimate or a local solver termination flag as success. A small enumerated grammar subset can provide structural coverage, while constant-fit uncertainty remains separate.

3. Make the representation a simplified graph and share structure where measured useful

The symbolic-regression survey discusses graph representations and reusable subexpressions; exhaustive SR explicitly removes parameter and functional redundancies. The current pilot counts every literal constant plus an output gain, even if an equivalent expression needs fewer independent values.

Our proposed extension. For example, g times c times sin(wt+b) needs one amplitude product, rather than two independent amplitude values. Apply safe, domain-preserving simplification and common-subexpression sharing before scoring a final payload. For a later whole-block extension, related modes may share a base phase: phi_k(t)=h_k phi_0(t)+delta_k(t). This is an explicit per-block program with shared nodes, not a shared trained waveform decoder.

Falsifiable experiment. Compare trees versus simplified trees versus reusable graphs; compare independent modes versus shared-phase graphs. Charge graph topology, references, harmonic multipliers and innovations. Re-evaluate after each rewrite. Do not assume all modes are harmonic or force sharing on unvoiced speech. Keep parameter-count and structural-description axes separate; RVQ is not required for this step.

4. Train decomposition against the cost of its actual reconstructed programs

VMD bandwidth, segmentation masks and downstream separation accuracy are different objectives from compact function reconstruction. A narrowband mode can still need a complicated envelope. Fixed eight-mode decomposition can also spend representation overhead unnecessarily.

Our proposed extension. After the fitter is reliable, use an alternating objective: D(x, sum_k f_k(t;theta_k)) + mu sum_k ||u_k-f_k||^2 + alpha BW(u) + lambda L(program graph). A learned VMD-like module produces u_k; generated programs f_k reconstruct the output. During a fixed-program step, optimize continuous decomposition and curve parameters. Periodically search, simplify, split, merge or deactivate modes and accept changes based on the actual evaluated description/error front.

Falsifiable experiment. Discrete program choices are not differentiable simply because the decomposition is. Start with alternating optimization and measured program costs; use a learned cost surrogate only with periodic exact-search checks. Compare against fixed VMD with the same strengthened fitter, matched total block size, and a direct-waveform program baseline. Charge or omit residuals explicitly, prevent uncharged raw-waveform bypasses, and monitor cancellation between large components and mode collapse.

What should count as success?

Use size at fixed error and error at fixed size, with coverage across all held-out modes. Report median/tail errors and success fractions, not only the easiest IMF or the mean. For each structure distinguish optimization quality from language/search failure with known-formula controls and stronger refitting. Track total fitting/search time and learned-model training time separately.

Parameter count is a useful current axis; complete description additionally includes operators, topology, shared references, support/knots and numerical values. Actual bitrates require a declared numeric representation and eventually decoding the encoded values. No RVQ or quantization experiment is proposed for the immediate stage.

Summing independently fitted mode errors introduces cross terms. Consequently, a good average per-mode NMSE does not certify the best reconstructed block. Evaluate and, where appropriate, jointly refit the summed output while keeping per-mode diagnostics.

MAPSS is informative about local distortion diagnostics but is not validated as a standalone 40-ms IMF quality score. Its human evidence uses 10-second separated signals, not frame-level judgments. Validate perceptual metrics later on reconstructed speech in consistent context; retain exact sampled error now.

A mathematical caveat in DAMD

This is our derivation, not a claim made by the paper. Theorem II.2 constructs hard disjoint partitions and then calls the resulting objective equivalent to unrestricted VMD. Constructing a feasible hard partition proves reconstruction and gives a restricted family; it does not prove an optimum among overlapping modes.

For one positive-frequency bin with value X, fixed center-distance weights a,b>0, unrestricted mode amplitudes satisfy:

min a|U1|² + b|U2|², subject to U1+U2=X
U1=bX/(a+b), U2=aX/(a+b)
Optimal cost = ab|X|²/(a+b) < min(a,b)|X|² (hard assignment)

A discrete three-bin example also separates the global hard optimum from a feasible soft decomposition. At frequencies [1,2,3] with spectral values [1,1,1] and two centers, exhaustive hard-cluster assignments have best cost 0.5. Keeping centers [1.5,3], soft mode values [0.94117647,0.8,0] and [0.05882353,0.2,1] sum exactly to the input and cost 0.43529412. Thus unrestricted soft optimization can beat the best hard partition in this discrete analogue. Common positive-frequency normalization factors do not change the inequality.

Practical implication: treat DAMD as a useful partitioning/initialization proposal, not an interchangeable optimization formulation. The paper itself later distinguishes frequency-domain partitioning from a generative time-domain intrinsic mode model. This caveat does not negate its empirical results.

Order of experiments

  1. Audit fitting and simplify redundant parameters with fixed modes; establish synthetic controls and practical compute/error curves.
  2. Compare direct, amplitude-phase and quadrature function discovery, adding residual-conditioned edits and retrieval as independently switchable ablations.
  3. Evaluate simplified graphs and shared per-block structure against independent programs, with actual original-signal reconstruction.
  4. Only then alternate learned VMD-like decomposition with actual program fitting and size-aware split/merge decisions.

The immediate research question remains function generation. Every proposal requires an elementary operator language and bounded computation. Learning composition avoids a menu of complete families; it does not certify the globally shortest function among all possible mathematics.

Paper-by-paper evidence and proposals

P01 | Variational generalized nonlinear mode decomposition: Algorithm and applications

Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing | 2024 | Focused page references: 2-8, 11, 15-16, 19

Method. Generalizes modes to time-domain chirps and frequency-domain dispersive components with explicit support intervals. Fuses time-frequency maps, clusters ridges, identifies coordinate direction, and initializes variational demodulation or dispersion compensation.

Evidence in the paper. Synthetic mixtures and noise tests; bat echolocation and railway vibration applications. The paper explicitly assumes separated time-frequency ridges and says its method fails when crossing/close ridges violate that condition.

Limits for our question. No compact parameter transmission or speech coding experiment. Demodulated amplitude/phase trajectories remain functions, not automatically short vectors. The ridge classification and thresholds are algorithmic choices.

Our proposed transfer. Generate programs for demodulated quadrature envelopes or phase innovations; test a frequency-domain representation for transient modes. Count carrier, support boundaries, coordinate choice, and envelope coefficients. Select by achieved description/error, not ridge-type rules alone.

P02 | TFD-Net: Towards Intelligent Time-Frequency Mode Decomposition with Practical Applications

AAAI | 2026 | Focused page references: 2-7; Fig. 1 on 3

Method. Complex multi-resolution convolutional transforms and residual U-Net features feed a query-based segmentation model. Hungarian matching supervises individual instantaneous-frequency tracks. Extracted tracks initialize a separate iterative demodulation reconstruction procedure.

Evidence in the paper. 30,000 training, 1,000 validation and 200 test synthetic modulated signals, length 256, one to six modes. BCE/Dice losses supervise maps and masks. Real examples are power-system oscillations and bat echolocation.

Limits for our question. The existing training does not optimize the size of a transmitted expression. Its conclusion identifies unfolded reconstruction as future work; it should not be described as already a fully neural end-to-end waveform decomposition.

Our proposed transfer. Borrow permutation-invariant query slots and frequency-track proposals to initialize VMD-like optimization. Add actual program reconstruction and complexity objectives; validate on speech and variable mode counts.

P03 | FINER: Flexible spectral-bias tuning in Implicit NEural Representation by Variable-periodic Activation Functions

Local arXiv v1 manuscript; FINER conference line | 2023 local version | Focused page references: 3-8

Method. Uses sin((abs(x)+1)*x) and wider bias initialization to expose different oscillation scales while retaining the base weight initialization. Studies spectral bias through geometry and empirical neural tangent kernels.

Evidence in the paper. Image fitting, signed-distance fields and radiance fields. Image comparison uses three hidden layers with 256 neurons each; varying bias initialization changes recoverable detail.

Limits for our question. This PDF is the original FINER preprint, not FINER++. Its reported improvements do not establish few-constant IMF coding, nor a generally optimal functional family. Initialization still affects convergence.

Our proposed transfer. Use variable-periodic composition as an optional expression primitive and fit small explicit programs, with initialization as a separate factor. Compare after expanding the primitive into elementary operations so it does not receive an artificially cheap code.

P04 | Recent Advances in Symbolic Regression

ACM Computing Surveys | 2025 | Focused page references: 4-12, 14-18, 24-25; category survey

Method. Surveys deterministic enumeration/programming, genetic programming, semantic search, neural generators, equation learners and hybrids. Distinguishes trees, graphs, grammar and reusable subexpressions; discusses accuracy, interpretability and computation separately.

Evidence in the paper. A methodological survey, not a new speech experiment. Describes variable projection, semantic replacement, graph reuse, neural guidance and sparsity-based equation learning across the literature.

Limits for our question. A survey description is not independent experimental validation of each cited method. General benchmark success cannot establish compact speech representations or global optimality of numerical fits.

Our proposed transfer. Replace isolated complete-tree proposals with neural-guided local edits and a reusable program graph. Benchmark generator, fitter and grammar independently, with both proposal and wall-time budgets.

P05 | Density-Based Adaptive Mode Decomposition

IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing | 2026 | Focused page references: 2-8, 10-12; Theorem II.2 on 4

Method. Uses weighted spectral meanshift with bandwidth selection to determine frequency partitions and mode counts. Direct extraction uses disjoint masks; optional hybrid refinement initializes VMD from clustering results.

Evidence in the paper. Synthetic signals and real examples including TIMIT speech. Reported timing: direct extraction 2.4 s, hybrid refinement 20.7 s, VMD 14.0 s for the stated comparison. Discussion calls the method a frequency-domain partitioning framework rather than a generative intrinsic time-domain mode model.

Limits for our question. Perfect reconstruction of uncompressed transform partitions is not parameter compression. The unrestricted VMD/clustering equivalence is not established by constructing hard partitions; see our algebraic counterexample below. Number of clusters remains sensitive to representation and bandwidth.

Our proposed transfer. Use density clustering as an initializer and candidate split/merge proposal, then accept mode counts based on actual compact-program reconstruction. Keep hard masks and soft VMD updates as distinct ablation arms.

P06 | FINER++: Building a Family of Variable-periodic Functions for Activating Implicit Neural Representation

IEEE TPAMI, accepted author version | 2026 | Focused page references: 5-9, 13-14

Method. Extends variable-periodic construction to sine, Gaussian and complex wavelet activations. Bias range controls the used portion of the activation; discusses overlap/degeneration and progressive streamable representations.

Evidence in the paper. Visual signal fitting and rendering, including a progressively widened network with earlier parameters frozen. A representative network has three layers and 256 neurons per layer. The author-version header says final edited content may change.

Limits for our question. The local PDF is FINER++, separate from P03. Extra initialization and scale parameters remain. Visual accuracy and a streamable neural network do not establish a compact symbolic IMF representation.

Our proposed transfer. Learn or search compact compositions with chirped/localized primitives, and progressively refine only unresolved structure. Preserve a fully explicit evaluable program; charge every per-IMF weight if an INR is used directly.

P07 | KAN: Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks

ICLR | 2025 | Focused page references: 3-6, 32, 46-47

Method. Learns spline functions on graph edges with summation nodes; supports grid refinement, sparsification, pruning and subsequent symbolic substitution.

Evidence in the paper. Mathematical/physical regression and PDE examples. The approximation theorem assumes sufficiently smooth compositional edge functions. Appendix M fits affine transforms around user-specified symbolic functions via grid search and regression.

Limits for our question. KAN does not automatically output an arbitrary shortest symbolic function. Its symbolification uses a user-defined candidate library. Splines, grids and weights have storage cost; for one input variable, the multivariate representation theorem is not the core advantage.

Our proposed transfer. Use a small adaptive spline/compositional network as a teacher to expose useful structure, then distill into explicit programs and re-evaluate. Also keep sparse splines as a direct, fully costed representation.

P08 | RAG-SR: Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Neural Symbolic Regression

ICLR | 2025 | Focused page references: 4-9, 11, 17-18

Method. Semantic descent removes a feature's contribution and generates/retrieves a replacement from the residual. A dynamic library stores evaluated subtrees and their outputs; a neural generator conditions on residual and a retrieved expression.

Evidence in the paper. Synthetic tree generation and 120 black-box regression datasets; separate Feynman/Strogatz results in appendix. Default evolutionary model has ten trees, population 200 and 100 generations. Retrieval restricts replacement size; full RAG-SR is slower than TPSR in their implementation.

Limits for our question. The model is multi-feature symbolic regression, not short speech IMF coding. Edit distance to a generating tree is not our primary objective. Residual addition without replacement can bloat descriptions.

Our proposed transfer. Condition expression edits on the current curve and its residual, not only raw IMF samples. Reuse good subexpressions, replace rather than blindly append, and retrain on actual successful speech-derived fits. Keep test speakers out of the training library.

P09 | Symbolic Regression via MDLformer-Guided Search: From Minimizing Prediction Error to Minimizing Description Length

ICLR | 2025 | Focused page references: 3-7, 10, 15-16

Method. Learns a predictor of simplified formula token length from numeric observations, with numeric/symbolic alignment pretraining, then uses it to guide search for useful subformulas.

Evidence in the paper. 133 known-formula and 122 black-box problems. Appendix: about 131 million generated data/formula pairs, 31.9M numeric-model parameters, 41 hours on eight A100 GPUs.

Limits for our question. Its MDL target is simplified symbolic length, not a measured audio payload including numerical precision. SymPy-based simplification is not proof of the globally shortest representation. Estimated complexity may fail out of distribution; it is a guide, not an optimality certificate.

Our proposed transfer. Train a smaller speech-specific predictor of remaining error-versus-description cost from actual search archives. Periodically verify rankings using the real fitter, retain uncertainty/exploration, and never accept a claimed compression result solely from the surrogate.

P10 | MAPSS: Manifold-based Assessment of Perceptual Source Separation

ICLR | 2026 | Focused page references: 2-6, 8-10, 17-19, 43

Method. Embeds references, distorted references and separated outputs with pretrained audio representations and diffusion maps. Defines distinct perceptual match and separation scores with uncertainty/truncation analyses.

Evidence in the paper. SEBASS: over 900 separated signals, about 11,000 ratings, 10-second outputs judged by 15 raters. No new listening study; fine-frame outputs are aggregated to compare with utterance-level human scores.

Limits for our question. Not validated as an isolated-IMF or isolated-40-ms speech-codec quality oracle. P11 | Coupled Splines for Sparse Curve Fitting

IEEE Transactions on Image Processing | 2022 | Focused page references: 2-5, 7-8; Theorems 2-3 and Fig. 3

Method. Fits two coordinate functions with rotation-invariant total-variation/group regularization of derivatives. Shared sparse knots and hybrid spline orders permit smooth regions and corners. Proves spline representer results for its stated periodic curve-fitting objective.

Evidence in the paper. 2D contour experiments; equal-knot comparison in Fig. 3 reports QFE 12.09 versus 153.47. Hybrid linear/cubic fits reduce knot counts in demonstrated contours.

Limits for our question. Theorems concern the declared 2D periodic convex objective, not arbitrary AM-FM factorization or shortest speech programs. Nonperiodic speech boundaries and sparse knot payloads need separate treatment.

Our proposed transfer. Fit the real and imaginary parts of a demodulated analytic IMF as coupled envelopes with shared adaptive knots. Phase rotation rotates these coordinates, suggesting a natural use of rotation-invariant coupling. Count knot locations, both coefficient sets and carrier parameters.

P12 | Learnable Empirical Mode Decomposition Based on Mathematical Morphology

SIAM Journal on Imaging Sciences; local author manuscript | 2022 | Focused page references: 6-14, 16, 18-20

Method. Replaces interpolation envelopes with dilation/erosion, opening/closing or Lasry-Lions constructions; learns structuring functions and convex envelope weights. Derives gradients through extrema and discusses additive-shift invariance of sifting.

Evidence in the paper. Toy 1D classification and hyperspectral classification (Indian Pines/Pavia), spatially disjoint train/test regions, ten repetitions. In the reported task the classifier uses residuals of a single decomposition step.

Limits for our question. This demonstrates trainable morphological preprocessing, not compact speech reconstruction. Nonsmooth extrema have sparse gradients, and the representation need not satisfy classical IMF criteria merely because the module is called EMD.

Our proposed transfer. Use constrained learnable envelopes as an alternative decomposition arm. Validate sum/remainder, oscillatory properties and actual formula cost, and impose no uncharged raw-signal feature bypass.

P13 | Neural Networks Fail to Learn Periodic Functions and How to Fix It

NeurIPS | 2020 | Focused page references: 2-6, 8-9

Method. Introduces Snake_a(x)=x+sin(a*x)^2/a, studies periodic extrapolation, monotonicity and initialization, and discusses learnable frequency.

Evidence in the paper. Synthetic periodic regression and other forecasting/classification examples. The extrapolation theorem is an existence result with sufficient width, not a finite-width optimization guarantee.

Limits for our question. This is the original Snake paper, not a SnakeBeta paper. Extrapolation beyond a fitting interval differs from our exact sampled reconstruction task. A favorable neural activation does not establish the smallest standalone IMF formula.

Our proposed transfer. Allow Snake-like residual-periodic compositions as language elements, compare against their expanded elementary formulas, and separately test trainability versus final achievable error at matched parameter counts.

P14 | Plug-and-play learnable EMD module for time-domain speech separation

Applied Acoustics | 2024 | Focused page references: 2-6

Method. Uses max pooling on positive/negative signals and transposed convolutions for EMD-inspired features, which supplement an existing time-domain speech-separation encoder.

Evidence in the paper. Four two-speaker separation datasets and SI-SNR-based training; small extra model/compute cost. Feature analysis acknowledges learned outputs are not a complete implementation of original EMD envelopes.

P15 | (Exhaustive) symbolic regression and model selection by minimum description length

Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A | 2026 | Focused page references: 3-7, 12-13

Method. Enumerates expressions within a declared operator set and maximum tree size, removes equivalences, fits constants, and ranks descriptions using structural, parameter-precision and residual terms.

Evidence in the paper. Benchmark and astrophysical applications. Describes a typical complexity-10 run of roughly 200 CPU hours. Shows different syntactic/parametric expressions can represent the same function; numerical tolerance still affects fitting.

Limits for our question. Enumeration coverage is bounded by grammar and maximum complexity; local constant fitting does not become globally solved through enumeration. MDL depends on coding/model assumptions and operator vocabulary. Its statistical precision formula should not be transplanted blindly to noiseless speech coding.

Our proposed transfer. Add safe canonical simplification, remove redundant scale/constant parameters, and enumerate very small programs as a coverage reference. Keep parameter-count frontiers now and report actual representation cost separately; precision-aware coding can follow later without RVQ.

P18 | Sinusoidal Frequency Estimation by Gradient Descent

ICASSP | 2023 | Focused page references: 1-4; Fig. 1 and Eq. 2 on 2

Method. Optimizes real parts of complex powers, Re(z^n), allowing radial damping while the frequency angle moves; uses amplitude correction/refitting to recover a real sinusoid.

Evidence in the paper. N=4096, zero initial target phase, 50k steps for single tones and 300k for mixtures in the reported settings. Improves over direct real sinusoidal optimization; authors observe component collisions and missing quieter tones.

Limits for our question. No formal general convergence result; not yet a general speech/chirped-AM-FM solution. Final error must be measured on the intended real expression, not the damped surrogate.

Our proposed transfer. Use damping as a temporary optimization aid, then restore/refit the transmitted real expression. Test random phases, chirps, weak components and 640-sample signals at practical budgets before adopting it.

P19 | Differentiable Time-Varying Linear Prediction in the Context of End-to-End Analysis-by-Synthesis

Interspeech | 2024 | Focused page references: 1-4

Method. Derives efficient sample-wise time-varying LP backpropagation and integrates it with a source-filter synthesizer. Studies source/noise-filter coupling and reflection coefficient parameterization.

Evidence in the paper. 24-kHz VCTK, 2-second training segments, one million optimization steps. Subjective comparison uses two selected speakers, with 17 retained listeners; phase and pitch estimation affect results.

A B S T R A C T
This paper presents a learnable empirical mode decomposition (EMD) module tailored for single channel speech
separation. The proposed EMD module exhibits a ﬂexible plug-and-play characteristic, enabling its integration
into various speech separation networks. Conventionally, non-learnable EMD algorithms, as a nonlinear timedomain adaptive techniques, aimed at decomposing speech signals into a set of oscillatory intrinsic mode
functions (IMFs) and a residual component. However, the inherent non-diﬀerentiability of the interpolation
operation in these algorithms restricts their integration into neural networks, thus the EMD algorithm only exists
in the preprocessing step and cannot be integrated into the neural network to form an end-to-end trainable speech
separation network. In order to mitigate this issue, we use the max pooling layer and the deconvolution network
to replace the envelope solving algorithm in the EMD method and build a trainable EMD neural network module.
Multiple sets of experiments have shown that the integrating of EMD module brings the improvement to diﬀerent
separation systems.

1. Introduction
Speech separation and speech enhancement are both basic tasks for
speech transcription and teleconferencing system, hearing aid, etc. In
recent times, signiﬁcant advancements have been made in the realm of
speech separation and enhancement, primarily through the utilization
of deep learning techniques [36,44]. In particular, deep learning-based
time-domain speech separation has emerged as a topic of active research
[22,23,20,21,42,7,19,6,18,37,40,25].
For single-channel multi-speaker speech separation task, the ConvTasNet network [23] has shown successful application potential. It utilizes a 1-D convolutional layer in lieu of the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) operation as a speech encoder for producing a learned
latent feature representation, thus mitigating the challenges associated
with phase reconstruction. The dual-path RNN (DPRNN) comprises two
types of recurrent neural networks: an intra-chunk RNN and an interchunk RNN [21]. Together, these networks repeatedly analyze sequential features at both the local and global levels. The dual-path Transformer network (DPTNet) [6] introduces an improved Transformer [34]
into DPRNN for modeling the speech sequences with direct contextawareness. The top-down attention network (TDANet) emulates the topdown attention mechanism observed in the human brain during speech
separation tasks, demonstrating promising prospects. In order to capture
nuanced structural information within speech samples, the utilization

of a graph convolutional network is extended to speech separation task
[37]. Notwithstanding, prevailing speech separation networks exhibit a
notable oversight by disregarding the intrinsic attributes of the temporal
domain signal, speciﬁcally the envelope feature.
In the realms of physics and engineering, the envelope of an oscillatory signal assumes a pivotal role, manifesting as a discernibly smooth
curve that delineates the signal’s extremities [12]. Fundamentally, the
envelope functions to expand and enhance the traditional concept of a
constant amplitude by bestowing upon it the property of instantaneous
amplitude. This nuanced conceptualization broadens the understanding
of signal dynamics, particularly within the context of oscillatory phenomena. And a burgeoning body of research has shown the slowly varying temporal component of speech (“envelope”) is essential for speech
understanding [28,2,17,26,3].
In fact, within the evolution of signal processing technology over
recent decades, envelope analysis methodologies have consistently featured prominently in both the realm of speech and non-speech signals.
The well-known method is the Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD)
[11] and it constitutes a sophisticated technique employed for the analysis of non-linear and non-stationary signals. The noteworthy attribute
of EMD is its eﬃcacy in decomposing time-series data into distinct components denoted as Intrinsic Mode Functions (IMFs). This attribute has
garnered widespread accept across diverse disciplines, notably within
the domain of speech processing. And EMD is esteemed for its aptitude

Combined with some frequency domain analysis methods, such as

the improvement of learnable EMD in time-domain speech separation.

quasi-interpolant through the utilization of deep neural networks. In
contrast to the above approach, our methodology entails the utilization
of both the maximum pooling layer and the deconvolution network to
supplant the conventional envelope solving algorithm within the EMD
framework. Consequently, we construct a trainable EMD neural network module, thereby introducing a novel paradigm in the treatment
of speech separation challenges.

5.2.2. Can EMD module be replaced by a simple convolutional network?
Increasing the number of model parameters has the potential to
enhance its performance. To determine whether the improvement in
performance achieved by the EMD module is solely due to the expansion of parameters, we employ one-dimensional convolutional networks
as a substitute for the EMD module and carry out several comparative
experiments. The outcomes of our research are summarized in Table 2.
Through comparative analysis, it is evident that the model preﬁxed
with “ENC” exhibits inferior performance relative to the model preﬁxed
with “EMD” and even underperforms compared to the original model.
Therefore, it can be inferred that the EMD module does not introduce
additional model parameters or uses the smaller kernel size but captures
more profound features.

6. Conclusion
In this work, we introduce a novel EMD module that can achieve
plug-and-play eﬀect in existing speech separation systems. The EMD
neural network module originates from the empirical mode decomposition method, and experiments have also shown that the learnable EMD
module has the advantages to establish a cutting-edge speech separation
model. We also have analyzed what the feature 𝑓̂ and 𝑓̌ represents and
they are strongly correlated with the key envelope points of the original
signal. But we have to admit that there are many improved versions of
the EMD algorithm, which this article did not explore. Are there more
EMD module structures that can be expanded? All of these questions
will lead our future research.
